## 🎯 Purpose of this Study

Lower lip reconstruction following cutaneous malignancy excision (primarily squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma) requires preserving oral competence, full lip length, and vermilion border alignment while minimizing surgical scarring. While standard primary reconstructions (wedge resections or advancement flaps) offer immediate wound closure, they frequently cause microstomia (lip shortening), contour deformity, and conspicuous scarring.

This study aims to:
- Introduce and evaluate a novel, minimally invasive surgical dressing modification: **sutured foam-assisted secondary intention healing (SIH)** during the critical first postoperative week.
- Compare clinical, functional, and aesthetic outcomes across three modalities: conventional SIH, foam-assisted SIH, and primary surgical reconstruction.
- Provide a safe, low-morbidity reconstructive alternative for large partial-thickness defects (involving ≥50% of lip width) in elderly or medically complex surgical patients.

---

## 🔬 Overview & Rationale

Conventional secondary intention healing of the lower lip is hampered during the initial 7 days by constant perioral mobility, saliva maceration, food contamination, bleeding, and dressing displacement.

## 🛠️ Methodological Features

<div class="method-grid">
  
  <div class="method-card">
    <div class="method-icon">📐</div>
    <h4>Sutured Multi-Layer Bolster Protocol</h4>
    <p>Application of a non-adherent silicone interface (Mepitel) coupled with high-absorption polyurethane foam (Mepilex), anchored circumferentially with perimeter non-absorbable sutures for exactly 7 days.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="method-card">
    <div class="method-icon">🔬</div>
    <h4>Pathology-Calibrated Defect Dimensioning</h4>
    <p>Accurate wound surface area calculations derived from formalin-fixed pathology measurements utilizing a standardized 1.25 tissue-shrinkage linear correction coefficient.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="method-card">
    <div class="method-icon">📊</div>
    <h4>Tri-Arm Comparative Design</h4>
    <p>Systematic retrospective comparison across 12 consecutive patients stratified into conventional SIH (n=3), foam-assisted SIH (n=4), and primary surgical flap/wedge reconstruction (n=5).</p>
  </div>

  <div class="method-card">
    <div class="method-icon">👄</div>
    <h4>Multi-Dimensional Morbidity Evaluation</h4>
    <p>Comprehensive tracking of early postoperative complications (bleeding, pain, mastication/speech difficulties) alongside long-term functional endpoints (lip length, scarring, vermilion margin continuity).</p>
  </div>

</div>

## 💡 Translational & Clinical Significance

1. **Minimizing Procedural Burden in Fragile Patients**: Eliminates the need for complex flap undermining or general anesthesia, providing a rapid, low-stress surgical option for elderly or medically compromised individuals.
2. **Superior Functional Preservation (No Microstomia)**: Avoids wedge resection-induced transverse lip shortening, maintaining complete oral competence and natural vermilion symmetry even in defects exceeding half of the lip.
3. **Optimized Margin Safety**: By keeping the wound bed naturally accessible beneath a temporary bolster, any secondary re-excision required for close or positive margins can be performed without disrupting complex flap architecture.
